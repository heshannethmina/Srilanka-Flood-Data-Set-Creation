\begin{abstract}
We propose a terrain-aware multimodal spatiotemporal graph neural network (TF-STGNN) for calibrated 24-hour-ahead flood probability forecasting at 51 nodes across 16 Sri Lankan river basins, trained on a leakage-controlled benchmark built entirely from public, key-free sources. The contribution spans four families: a \emph{flow-lagged relational attention} mechanism respecting multi-day channel travel times, a \emph{FiLM terrain fusion} strategy, a multi-relational \emph{graph representation}, and an \emph{onset-weighted training objective} distilled to single-network inference. Preliminary results confirm feasibility---the full ablation ladder and three evaluation protocols complete in 5.07\,h on one free-tier GPU---and already show the terrain-fused graph model exceeding a published STGCN baseline by $+0.079$ PR-AUC while detecting $1.8\times$ more flood episodes before onset.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{flood early warning, graph neural networks, attention mechanisms, probability calibration, data leakage}

\section{Problem Motivation}

Sri Lanka experiences recurrent monsoon-driven riverine flooding. The southwest monsoon (May--September) floods steep, fast-responding wet-zone basins (Kelani, Kalu, Gin, Nilwala); the northeast monsoon (December--February) floods dry-zone rivers. Events in 2003, 2011, 2016, 2017, 2021 and 2024 each displaced large populations.

\textbf{The requirement is difficult on three axes at once.} Warning needs \emph{lead time} (a forecast, not a nowcast), \emph{spatial specificity} (which river reach, not which province), and \emph{calibrated confidence}---an evacuation decision needs a probability that means what it says. Meeting all three under \emph{severe resource constraints} is the practical problem: a national system in a lower-middle-income country cannot depend on paid data feeds, in-situ telemetry at every reach, or sustained GPU capacity. Zero monetary cost and free-tier compute are therefore design constraints, not conveniences, and they motivate the efficiency contribution in Section~\ref{sec:novelty}.

\textbf{Who benefits.} The Disaster Management Centre and Irrigation Department, which currently issue basin-level rather than reach-level advisories; residents of the $\sim$1.2\,M-person Kelani floodplain; and the flood-ML community, for whom we release the first ML-ready Sri Lankan benchmark.

\textbf{Three failures recur in this literature} and shape our contribution: (1) spatiotemporal networks discard directed river flow for Euclidean proximity or grids, so messages propagate against the physics; (2) random cross-validation on autocorrelated data places days from one flood event in both train and test; (3) point predictions are uncalibrated and cannot support graded response.

\section{Identified Dataset}

We construct a multimodal node--day panel covering 51 nodes in 16 basins, 2003--2025. All sources are public, key-free and programmatically cached: \textbf{meteorology} (precipitation, temperature, humidity, wind, radiation, soil wetness) from Open-Meteo Archive (ERA5-Land) and NASA POWER; \textbf{discharge} from Copernicus GloFAS reanalysis; \textbf{terrain} from Copernicus DEM; \textbf{Sentinel-1 RTC SAR} via Microsoft Planetary Computer.

The panel holds 410,931 node-days $\times$ 67 features. After 30-day warm-up and causal truncation the modelled tensor is $8{,}036 \times 51 \times 33$ dynamic features plus 9 static terrain descriptors. \textbf{Labels:} flood state is $Q_v(t) \ge q_v^{(0.98)}$, the 98th percentile of training-period discharge at node $v$; the primary target is 24-hour-ahead flood probability, computed causally within node boundaries. The benchmark contains 1,469 episodes (mean 5.39 days, IQR 2--6).

\textbf{Graph:} 51 nodes; 35 directed upstream$\to$downstream edges (never symmetrised), 204 spatial $k$-NN edges ($k{=}4$, weight $\exp(-d/40\,\mathrm{km})$), 51 self-loops, held as three distinct relations. Edge attributes carry distance and elevation drop.

\textbf{Splits are precomputed, not random.} Temporal: train $\le$2017 (277,899 node-days, 2.028\% positive), validation 2018--2020 (55,896, 0.810\%), test 2021+ (74,463, 2.276\%). Basin holdout (Gin): 324,726/51,512/32,020. A random split is retained \emph{as a diagnostic only}, to quantify leakage.

\subsection{Model Architecture}

TF-STGNN compresses four input streams into one vector per node per day, then lets those vectors exchange information along the river network. \textbf{Stream 1 (weather and discharge)} passes a 14-day window of 33 dynamic channels---rainfall, discharge, soil moisture and their accumulations---through a 2-layer GRU (hidden 128, dropout 0.2) pooled by additive attention, so the model weights the days that mattered rather than simply the most recent. \textbf{Stream 2 (terrain)} maps the 9 static descriptors through a small MLP to a scale and shift that multiply and offset the temporal vector channel-wise (FiLM). Terrain is therefore never an extra input feature; it \emph{changes how the same rainfall is read}, which is the point---200\,mm on a steep upper catchment is not 200\,mm on a flat delta. \textbf{Stream 3 (SAR)}, used only in the multimodal variant, encodes a $2{\times}512{\times}512$ Sentinel-1 chip with a ResNet-18 into 64 dimensions, gated by a presence flag and an age-in-days scalar so fusion can distinguish ``no observation'' from ``no water''. A two-layer MLP (GELU, LayerNorm) reduces the result to a 128-dimensional node state.

\textbf{Stream 4 (river graph)} runs two relational GATv2~\cite{GATv2} layers (4 heads $\times$ 32 dims) over the 51 nodes. Attention is computed \emph{separately per relation}---directed flow, distance-weighted proximity, self-loops---with edge attributes carrying distance and elevation drop, so an upstream neighbour and a merely nearby neighbour are never conflated. The graph output is concatenated with the pre-graph state, a skip path that lets an unseen basin bypass message passing, and read out by an MLP (256$\to$128) into four classification heads (flood at 1/2/3 days, onset at 1 day) and two regression heads (next-day discharge percentile, forward 3-day maximum discharge $z$-score). Logits are divided by a temperature fitted on validation, yielding calibrated probabilities. \textbf{The baseline M0 is this network with streams 2--4 removed}---temporal encoder and heads only, 294,727 parameters---so that each later step adds exactly one component. We also implement \textbf{STGCN}~\cite{STGCN2018} as a published spatiotemporal-GNN reference and retain LightGBM as a \emph{non-neural} bar for the operational discharge-percentile rule.

\subsection{Implementation Details}

One training example is a full-graph day snapshot; batches hold 32 such snapshots. Optimisation uses Adam ($\text{lr}=10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), gradient clipping at 1.0, 5 warm-up epochs and up to 60 epochs with early stopping (patience 10). Loss is a weighted multitask sum---$1.0$ on the 24-hour head, $0.3$ on the 2/3-day heads, $0.5$ on onset, $0.2$ on the regression heads---with focal weighting ($\alpha{=}0.75$, $\gamma{=}2$) scaled by per-label confidence to handle the 2\% positive rate. Normalisation statistics are fitted on the training block only; calibration (temperature or isotonic/PAVA) and the decision threshold are fitted on validation only.

\textbf{Hyperparameter tuning} searches GRU hidden $\in\{64,128,256\}$, layers $\in\{1,2\}$, dropout $\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$, lr $\in\{10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3}\}$ and lookback $\in\{7,14,30\}$\,days (default 14), selecting on \emph{validation episode-level} PR-AUC. Episode-level rather than node-day selection is essential: the 2018--2020 validation block is hydrologically quiet (0.810\% positive versus 2.276\% in test), which makes node-day PR-AUC a noisy and biased model-selection signal.

\subsection{Baseline Experimental Results}
\label{sec:baseline}

Table~\ref{tab:baselines} establishes the two facts that scope the project. First, \textbf{the ranking task is near-saturated by autocorrelation}: a lookup on today's discharge percentile, with no learning, reaches 0.8164 PR-AUC, because most positive node-days are continuations of an ongoing flood rather than onsets, leaving PR-AUC little headroom above a trivial rule. Second, \textbf{the operational task is far from saturated}: the strongest ranker warns before onset on only 16.1\% of episodes, and the best any baseline achieves is 22.3\%. (Climatology's 0.515 is an artefact of alarming almost constantly, FAR $=0.882$, and is not skill.) We therefore adopt \textsc{ev.det} at comparable FAR as the primary criterion and PR-AUC as secondary; ROC-AUC saturates at 0.98--0.99 across all configurations and is uninformative here.

\section{Proposed Contribution}

\subsection{Expected Novelty}
\label{sec:novelty}

\textbf{Architecture---novel attention mechanism (primary).} A \emph{flow-lagged relational attention} layer. In standard spatiotemporal GNNs a node attends to its neighbours' representations on the \emph{same} day. For river networks this is physically wrong: water requires hours to days to propagate downstream, so zero-lag message passing attends to the correct neighbours at the wrong time. We attach to each directed flow edge a travel-time offset $\tau_{uv}$---derived from channel distance and elevation drop, or learned per edge with a differentiable soft-gather over the temporal axis---so that node $v$ attends to $u$'s state at $t-\tau_{uv}$. Spatial edges remain at zero lag. To our knowledge, lag-parameterised attention over a directed hydrological graph has not been reported.

\textbf{Architecture---fusion strategy.} FiLM~\cite{FiLM2018} terrain conditioning (Section~3.1), identity-initialised so training begins at M0. Already validated (Section~\ref{sec:improve}).

\textbf{Representation---graph.} Flow, proximity and self-loops as independently parameterised relations, with flow never symmetrised---unlike most published flood GNNs.

\textbf{Learning---novel training strategy.} An \emph{onset-weighted} objective. Because $\sim$80\% of positive node-days are flood continuations, the gradient is dominated by examples persistence already solves; we weight each positive by recency to onset, concentrating capacity on the transition warning actually requires.

\textbf{Efficiency---inference cost.} Calibration currently needs a 5-seed ensemble~\cite{Lakshminarayanan2017}, a $5\times$ inference cost incompatible with Section~1. We will distil it into one network and test whether calibration survives.

\textbf{Already confirmed.} Table~\ref{tab:ladder} validates the fusion contribution: FiLM terrain conditioning (M0$\to$M1) yields $+0.105$ PR-AUC and $+0.062$ \textsc{ev.det}, the largest single step in the ladder. Calibration behaves as designed---focal loss alone drives ECE to 0.0756, and ensembling with temperature scaling~\cite{Guo2017} restores it to 0.0088 while adding $+0.033$ PR-AUC. Against STGCN, M5 gains $+0.079$ PR-AUC at \emph{lower} FAR, and every configuration from M1 onward exceeds every baseline on \textsc{ev.det} (Section~\ref{sec:baseline}) by up to $1.8\times$.

\textbf{Evidence motivating the attention contribution.} Adding directed flow edges at zero lag (M2$\to$M3) \emph{degrades} every test metric. We read this not as evidence that topology is uninformative but as a mechanism failure: attention over a 35-edge flow relation cannot help when it samples upstream state at a physically impossible instant. This is the hypothesis the lagged mechanism tests, and the null gives it a controlled comparison point. SAR fusion likewise falls within seed noise, expected with imagery at 9 of 51 nodes. M0--M4 are single-seed, so the M2--M3 gap is not yet statistically resolvable; a 5-seed re-run is the immediate next experiment.

\textbf{Expected gains.} We target \textsc{ev.det} $>0.50$ at FAR $\le 0.25$ from lagged attention plus onset weighting (versus 0.408 at FAR 0.315 now), ECE $<0.02$ from a single distilled network, and $5\times$ lower inference cost than the current ensemble.

The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:ladder} shows why this evaluation discipline matters: a random split raises PR-AUC from 0.704 to 0.905 while pre-onset detection \emph{collapses} from 0.380 to 0.089. Leakage does not merely inflate a score---it inverts model selection, so tuning on a random split systematically prefers networks that recognise floods already underway and warn nobody.

\section{Experimental Plan}

\textbf{Evaluation metrics.} \emph{Ranking:} PR-AUC (primary), ROC-AUC (comparability only). \emph{Calibration:} Brier with Murphy decomposition, ECE (15 bins), reliability diagrams. \emph{Operational:} POD, FAR, CSI at validation-selected thresholds. \emph{Episode-level:} \textsc{ev.det}, the proportion of the 1,469 episodes alarmed at least once in the 7 days strictly before onset, plus mean lead time; an episode detected once counts once, which is what an operator experiences. \emph{Efficiency:} parameters, per-epoch time, inference latency.

\textbf{Ablations} isolate each contribution: lag ($0$ / distance-derived $\tau$ / learned $\tau$), fusion (none / concat / FiLM), relation set (none / spatial / flow / both), loss (BCE / focal / onset-weighted), calibration (raw / temperature / ensemble / distilled).

\textbf{Baselines.} Deep: per-node GRU (M0), per-node LSTM, STGCN, and a zero-lag relational GATv2 (the direct ablation of the proposed mechanism). Non-neural reference: persistence, per-node climatology, discharge-percentile rule, LightGBM.

\textbf{Resource requirements.} Parameters range from 294,727 (M0) through 581,319 (graph variants) to 11,800,199 with the SAR CNN. The complete suite ran in \textbf{5.07\,h on a single Tesla T4} (Kaggle free tier); per-epoch cost is 4.2\,s (M0), 13.5\,s (graph) and 94\,s (SAR CNN). The lagged mechanism adds one gather per flow edge, leaving cost within $\sim$10\% of M3. Storage: $\sim$40\,MB tabular, $\sim$1.4\,GB imagery. Monetary cost is zero.
